## The idea

Most survival games put warmth in a bar. Emberline puts it on the ground.

A furnace at the centre of camp thaws a circle of frozen earth. The radius of that circle *is* the heat. Feed the fire and the world opens; let it burn low and it closes in. There is no meter to watch, because the meter is the ground you stand on.

## The loop

Wood is the ordinary fuel. It regrows slightly slower than one person can carry it away, so a run is routing, not grinding.

Beyond the circle the snow is deep and slow. Crossable, deliberately — a hard edge would trap a player whose fire had burned low. A full furnace thaws exactly as far as the outermost trees, so "the fire is full" and "the whole forest is in reach" are the same sentence.

Everything worth having is out past that edge. Coal burns far hotter than wood. Hares are the only meat, and meat is the only thing that makes a night cheaper: the squad eats at dusk and fights harder for it.

The cold is not a wall, it is the price list. The map is generated fresh each run: the lesson stays the same and the ground never does.

## The night

Seven nights, each longer and colder. Each day brings its own weather, so no two runs press on you in the same order.

At dusk the gates light up — one at first, three by the finale. That is the only information the night gives you, and it never lies. You have one guard squad and five seconds to decide where it stands, and travel time is the price of choosing wrong. The squad fights on its own; if one gets past them, your pickaxe is the last word.

## Designed for a thumb

Portrait, one hand. The left thumb walks; two buttons sit under the right. A swings the pickaxe at whatever is nearest — tree, seam or wolf — so there is nothing to aim. B sprints on a stamina you can run dry. A tap on open ground rallies the squad to the nearest gate, so an imprecise thumb still gives the order you meant.

One line at the foot of the screen always names the next thing to do. It exists because a first playtester never worked out what the game wanted.

A run begins by holding the screen until the furnace catches: a filling circle, the game's own symbol before anything is explained. It opens with the outermost trees just past the thawed edge, so your first walk hits deep snow and the rule lands in one gesture.

## What it deliberately is not

No crafting tree, no inventory screen, no dialogue, no aiming. Nothing you gather is spent anywhere but the fire. Every system had to justify itself against the ring.

## What it is for

A ten-minute run that ends with you knowing which decision cost you the fire.
