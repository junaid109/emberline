## The idea

Most survival games put warmth in a bar. Emberline puts it on the ground.

A furnace at the centre of the camp thaws a circle of frozen earth. The radius of that circle *is* the heat. Feed the fire and the world opens; let it burn low and the world closes in around you. There is no meter to watch, because the meter is the ground you are standing on.

## The loop

Wood is the ordinary fuel. It grows at the treeline and comes back slightly slower than one person can carry it away, so a run is routing rather than grinding.

Beyond the thawed circle the snow is deep and you move at less than half speed. Crossable, deliberately — a hard edge would trap a player whose fire had burned low, with the trees they need on the far side. A full furnace thaws exactly as far as the outermost trees, so "the fire is full" and "the whole forest is in reach" are the same sentence.

Coal lies out there, always past that edge, and burns far hotter than wood. The better fuel always costs the cold walk, and boulders mean the way is rarely straight.

The forest, the seams and the rocks are generated fresh each run, so the lesson stays the same and the map never does.

## The night

Seven nights, each longer and colder than the last.

At dusk the gates light up — one at first, three by the finale. That is the only information the night gives you, and it never lies. You have one guard squad and five seconds to decide where it stands. Travel time is the price of choosing wrong. Combat resolves itself; you never aim. The decision is placement, made once, under a clock.

## Designed for a thumb

Portrait, one hand, three lines of instruction on the title card and none after it. The left thumb walks. A tap anywhere else rallies the squad to the nearest gate, so an imprecise thumb on a moving train still gives the order you meant.

A run begins by holding the screen until the furnace catches: a filling circle, the game's own symbol before anything is explained.

The game teaches itself through geometry. A run opens with the outermost trees just past the thawed edge, so your first walk hits deep snow and the rule lands in a single gesture — before any wolf exists to complicate it.

## What it deliberately is not

No crafting tree, no inventory screen, no dialogue, no aiming. Two fuels, one decision a night, one circle. Every system had to justify itself against the ring, and anything that would have read as a second meter was cut.

The palette follows the same discipline: the player is the only cold-bright thing on the field, the furnace the only warm one.

## What it is for

A ten-minute run that ends with you knowing exactly which decision cost you the fire.
